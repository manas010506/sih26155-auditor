## Measurement Methodology

We measure two different things, deliberately, to provide an honest assessment of the tool's capability.

**1. Rule Correctness (Seeded Corpus)**
We use a corpus of 37 configurations generated by injecting known misconfigurations into a hardened baseline. Because the injection is the ground truth, labels are correct by construction.
- **Result**: 100% detection of 74 seeded mutations.
- **Purpose**: This validates that every rule is syntactically correct and fires exactly when the target misconfiguration is present.

**2. Real-World Coverage (Held-Out Set)**
We collected 6 real-world Cisco IOS configurations from vendor documentation and public repositories, hand-labelled against CIS controls by a security engineer *before* the auditor was run on them.
- **Holdout Configs**: 6
- **Issues Labelled**: 31
- **Detected**: 24
- **Detection Rate**: 77.4%
- **False Positives**: 85
- **Purpose**: This measures what the tool would actually catch on a device it has never seen. 

### Analysis of Missed Issues
The 22.6% miss rate is primarily due to:
- **Parser Gaps**: Certain configuration patterns in real-world files (e.g., specific VTY auth styles) are not yet emitted by the parser.
- **Rule Strictness**: Some rules are tuned for the seeded corpus and are slightly too strict for organic real-world variance.

The gap between 100% (seeded) and 77.4% (real-world) is the most honest metric we can provide. It proves the rules work, while identifying the exact roadmap for parser improvements.
